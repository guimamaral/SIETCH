Happy Tuesday! I wanted to reflect and talk about my experience as a participant in Bluedot Impact's Technical AI Safety Course, specifically week one. 

Week one was all about visualizing what success means in AI Safety and why building AI safely is hard. To start, we discussed the the three main success outcomes in AI Safety. 

The first is to build AI slowly and safely. Personally, this is in my opinion the ideal success threshold. AI can enable much good in the world, and our best chance at that outcome relies on current frontier AI labs to focus on safety and security. This is obviously NOT the approach they are currently on. That is why I believe this success threshold is unlikely, especially considering that it assumes that no other frontier AI lab races ahead while the others are left addressing safety. 

The second success threshold is that frontier AI labs accept the race and push hard to improve their model capabilities as much as possible. This seems to be the path that we are currently on and considering that there is a geopolitical calculus here, namely China's push for AI dominance, is the most likely outcome. There are obvious flaws with this success threshold. Pushing hard on the margin might inadvertently make AI systems less safe because of the lack of time allocated for safety research and testing. Additionally, collective humanity has no established definition of "good" v.s "bad" actors.

The third success threshold and my personal favorite is to simply not build ASI-like systems. In my view, it is impossible that humans can effectively control systems/beings that are smarter than us. It's like trying to draw in a catfish using a pencil, it just doesn't make sense. I am in favor of indefinite moratoriums on frontier AI development until we can with 100% clarity make them safe for humanity. 

Irrespective of what outcome you most desire, it is a known fact that building AI safely is HARD. I learned that we're experimenting with systems we DO NOT understand, the goals we specify have flaws we DO NOT foresee, and AIs pursue goals in ways we don't expect. There are terms for the last two, namely reward misspecification and goal misgeneralization. 

I also learned that I have no idea what the difference between model understanding and model interpretability is, and which one is more important (and in what scenarios?). 

I don't think AI Safety researchers know either...
